Who we are

A Lae-based security company with a public track record

Kuima Security Services Ltd is identified in business listings and published reporting as a security company based in Lae, Morobe Province, Papua New Guinea. The company’s supplied business identity describes it as 100% nationally owned and uses the slogan “Your Security Is Our Business.”

Historical news reports provide evidence of work in different settings over time, including the Papua New Guinea University of Technology, port facilities and a mine-security joint venture arrangement. These reports describe past engagements and must not be read as confirmation that any contract remains active today.

Kuima’s current ownership particulars, leadership, active contracts, service coverage and licensing status should be confirmed directly with the company and the relevant Papua New Guinea authorities.

Publicly reported history

Selected milestones from published sources

The following is a concise summary of historical reporting—not a complete corporate history or a statement of current contract status.

🎓

University campus security

A 2013 report in The National said Kuima had provided security at the Papua New Guinea University of Technology in Lae for 12 years. It reported that the arrangement ended when the agreement lapsed in 2013.

Read the 2013 report ↗

⚓

Port and terminal security

A 2015 report stated that Kuima had renewed its contract with PNG Ports Corporation and described the company as having served the country’s ports and terminals for six consecutive years at that time. This is historical information.

Read the 2015 report ↗

⛏️

Mine-security joint venture

In March 2021, The National reported that Golpu-Wafi Security Services, a landowner-owned company, signed a three-year joint-venture agreement with Kuima for mine security. The report quoted a representative describing Kuima as Lae-based with mine-site experience.

Read the 2021 report ↗

🤝

Community contribution

Published reports also described Kuima supporting community members affected by a 2014 clash in Lae and donating uniforms to police officers in a 2017 report. These are examples documented in news coverage, not a claim about an ongoing programme.

Our working principles

What clients should be able to clarify

Security arrangements work best when expectations, responsibilities and compliance are established clearly before deployment.

Understand the assignment

Define the site, assets, hours, access points, operational risks and expected duties before requesting a proposal.

Clear communication

Agree on supervision, incident escalation, shift handovers, reporting and the main points of contact.

Verify current credentials

Before engagement, request current company and security-guard permits, relevant insurance and references, and verify licensing with the Security Industries Authority.
