Our HSE approach

Practical principles for responsible security work

These are proposed operating principles for security assignments, not a claim that Kuima holds a particular certification or has published a formally audited HSE management system. Site-specific procedures and current company policies must be confirmed before deployment.

🦺

Protect people

Promote safe conduct, situational awareness, suitable task-specific protective equipment and respectful treatment of workers, visitors and the public.

🔎

Identify hazards

Understand site induction requirements, restricted zones, traffic routes, lighting, weather, fatigue risks and other hazards relevant to the post.

📣

Report and escalate

Raise hazards, near misses, injuries, security incidents and unsafe conditions promptly through the agreed supervisor and client reporting channels.

🚑

Emergency readiness

Know the site’s alarm signals, emergency contacts, evacuation routes, assembly points and first-aid arrangements before duty begins.

🌿

Respect the environment

Avoid littering, unnecessary disturbance, spills and damage to vegetation or site assets. Follow the client’s waste, spill and environmental controls.

🤝

Work with the client

Coordinate with site management and authorised emergency responders, follow lawful site rules and maintain clear shift handovers and records.

Before and during a shift

Safe work starts with preparation

Security posts differ. Every assignment should be planned around the actual site hazards, duties, equipment and emergency arrangements.

Site induction

Confirm post orders, access restrictions, radio or phone procedures, emergency exits and who has authority to give instructions.

Fit for duty

Arrive prepared for the shift, follow fatigue-management arrangements, and report any condition that could make the assigned work unsafe.

Equipment and PPE

Check that assigned communications equipment and any required protective equipment are suitable and usable; report defects before relying on them.

Traffic and patrol safety

Use authorised routes and vehicle procedures, stay alert around moving plant and traffic, and avoid entering restricted or hazardous areas without approval.

Respectful interactions

Communicate calmly, protect privacy, avoid unnecessary confrontation and use the site’s escalation procedures when a situation becomes unsafe.

Records and handovers

Record relevant events factually and securely, and pass outstanding hazards or instructions to the next authorised person during handover.

These are general safety prompts. They do not replace client inductions, emergency plans, risk assessments, training or applicable legal duties.

When something goes wrong

Respond, report, review

Follow the site emergency plan and the instructions of authorised responders. Do not put yourself or others at additional risk.

Make the area safe

Raise the alarm and move to safety when needed. Do not attempt actions beyond your training or authority.

Contact the right people

Use the site’s emergency contacts and notify the designated supervisor or client representative as soon as it is safe.

Record the facts

Document the time, location, observations, actions and notifications according to the site’s reporting rules.

Support follow-up

Cooperate with authorised reviews so hazards can be addressed and suitable corrective actions considered.

Environmental responsibility

Protect the site and surrounding community

Security personnel can help protect environmental controls by following site rules, keeping observation areas tidy, reporting leaks or spills, avoiding disturbance of sensitive areas and promptly escalating suspected environmental harm.

At mining, industrial, port and construction sites, the client’s environmental management plan and emergency procedures determine the required actions. Personnel should not handle hazardous substances or attempt spill clean-up unless trained, equipped and authorised to do so.

Kuima’s specific environmental procedures, certifications and performance data were not independently established by the public sources reviewed for this page. Clients should request the current documents relevant to a proposed assignment.

Use official guidance and verify requirements

These government resources provide background on workplace safety and environmental regulation. They are links for reference, not legal advice.

🏛️

Department of Labour & Industrial Relations

The Department’s Occupational Safety & Health programme describes its role in workplace inspections, safety awareness and monitoring statutory OSH requirements.

Visit official OSH page ↗

📘

Industrial Safety, Health and Welfare Act 1965

The Department of Labour and Industrial Relations publishes this Act in its downloads area. Confirm with the Department which current laws and requirements apply to the particular workplace.

View official Act resource ↗

🌏

Conservation and Environment Protection Authority

CEPA publishes Papua New Guinea environmental policies and Acts, including the Environment Act 2000. Site-specific environmental obligations should be checked with the responsible client and competent authorities.

Legislation and regulatory guidance can change. Confirm the applicable current requirements with the relevant PNG authorities and qualified advisers.
