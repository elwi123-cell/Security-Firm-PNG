Our quality approach

Make expectations clear. Check performance. Learn from results.

Security assignments are different from site to site. Quality expectations should therefore be agreed with the client before work begins and reflected in assignment instructions, communication routes, reporting formats and review arrangements.

A practical quality approach may include checking that posts are covered as scheduled, handovers are completed, incidents are reported through agreed channels, and client feedback is considered. These are recommended practices for a security operation—not a claim that a particular internal quality management system or certification has been independently verified for Kuima.

Kuima Security Services Ltd is based in Lae, Morobe Province, Papua New Guinea. For any proposed engagement, clients should request and verify the current company documents, licences, insurance and service-specific procedures relevant to the assignment.

Quality in practice

What good service controls can cover

The following are useful areas to agree and review with the client for each assignment. They should be tailored to the actual contract and site.

📋

Defined post orders

Document the post’s duties, authorised actions, access rules, escalation contacts, shift times and limits of authority.

🕒

Attendance and handover

Agree how shift attendance, relief arrangements, handover notes and any service interruption will be recorded and communicated.

📡

Communication

Set clear radio or phone protocols, reporting times, contact lists and expectations for escalating urgent matters.

📝

Incident reporting

Use factual, timely reports that distinguish direct observations from information received and protect sensitive information.

🔍

Supervision and checks

Where included in the assignment, define supervisor visits, checklist items, review frequency and how identified issues are followed up.

🤝

Client feedback

Provide a clear channel for questions, complaints and improvement suggestions, with agreed acknowledgement and follow-up expectations.

These are examples of good-practice controls, not a representation that every item is currently implemented on every Kuima assignment.

A practical review cycle

Plan, deliver, check and improve

A simple cycle helps the client and service provider identify whether the agreed requirements are being met.

Agree requirements

Confirm the scope, duties, staffing expectations, reporting format, service hours and performance measures in writing.

Brief the team

Make sure relevant personnel receive the approved instructions, site induction information and escalation contacts.

Review evidence

Use appropriate logs, reports, checklists, client feedback and supervisory observations to assess the agreed requirements.

Follow up

Record issues, assign an appropriate owner, agree actions and check whether the response addressed the concern.

Measures to agree

Useful service indicators

Targets should be realistic, measurable and agreed in the contract. No performance figures are published here because verified Kuima-specific results were not established from the public sources reviewed.

Post coverage

Track scheduled coverage, approved relief arrangements and any gaps that need to be reported.

Reporting timeliness

Agree when routine reports and urgent incident notifications are due, and check completion against that agreement.

Issue resolution

Monitor whether reported concerns are acknowledged, assigned, addressed and closed out within agreed timeframes.

Training and induction

Verify assignment-specific induction and any required qualifications or permits before personnel undertake relevant duties.

Client satisfaction

Gather structured feedback through agreed meetings, reviews or documented feedback channels.

Record integrity

Keep records legible, factual, appropriately stored and available only to authorised people.
